ReLU activation vs SimpleGate

NAFNet drops the pointwise activation. Both are nonlinear, but the value distributions they produce are very different, and that is what matters for INT8.

ReLU: a fixed, clipping activation
A pointwise function, y = max(0, x). It bends at zero and clips every negative value.
The function
Output distribution for a normal input
Half the values are clipped to exactly 0 (the spike). One-sided, saturating.
SimpleGate: no activation, just a product
Split the channels in half (A, B) and multiply them: y = A × B. Nonlinear, but nothing is clipped.

Smooth and two-sided, centred on zero. No clipping, no hard floor.
ReLU saturates and clips, piling mass at zero and leaving a one-sided range that shifts under quantisation. SimpleGate keeps a smooth, symmetric, predictable range. That is why NAFNet quantises to INT8 and exports to the edge cleanly, while still being nonlinear.
